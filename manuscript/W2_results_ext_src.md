The dense quadratic subproblems of SQP grow with the cube of the number of parameters; L-BFGS-B works with bounds and a limited-memory Hessian approximation, so its cost grows roughly with the number of parameters times the number of iterations.

One practical detail matters for reproducibility. On the simulated problem with p = 30, L-BFGS-B stopped after a few iterations on its relative-reduction criterion at an objective {{num(UNGUARDED30 - CRUN('sim_p30')['rows']['reparam_lbfgsb']['obj'],3)}} above the optimum, although the projected gradient was far from zero. We therefore check the projected-gradient (KKT) condition after every run and restart L-BFGS-B from its last iterate while it exceeds 10⁻⁵; all results reported here use this safeguard, which was triggered in {{sum(DSX(ds)['monotonicity']['solver_restarted'] for ds in ['POL252','PARK195','BCW683','HEART303'])}} of the {{f"{sum(DSX(ds)['monotonicity']['solver_fits'] for ds in ['POL252','PARK195','BCW683','HEART303']):,}"}} fits of the cross-validation study and changed fold-level AUCs by up to {{num(max(DSX(ds)['monotonicity']['v1_max_abs_auc_change'] for ds in ['POL252','PARK195','BCW683','HEART303']),3)}} but mean AUCs by at most {{num(V1CHANGE,3)}}, leaving every conclusion unchanged.

## 7.6 Case study: the autism-screening model

For the screening model the two layers combine as in Corollary 1. The Mamdani layer is certified monotone in both inputs on its whole domain (Section 7.1); it could not be evaluated for discrimination on POL252, which records no age information, so the age-lag index is unavailable. In the fusion layer fitted on the four Q-CHAT domain scores of POL252 (communication–language, imitation–learning, personal–social and other atypical signs; an earlier run with seed 20260827 and the same protocol), the monotone 2-additive model reached an AUC of {{num(G3['cv_auc']['domain_graph']['mean'],4)}}, against {{num(G3['cv_auc']['domain_graph_unconstrained']['mean'],4)}} without constraints and {{num(G3['cv_auc']['domain_additive_monotone']['mean'],4)}} for the additive monotone model. At this level the constraints were never binding, whereas the unconstrained model violated (C) in {{pct(G3['monotonicity']['domain']['unconstrained_folds_nonmonotone'])}} of the folds; the monotone model selected almost no interaction (on average {{num(G3['monotonicity']['domain']['mean_edges'],1)}} edges), and its normalized Shapley values were {{num(G3['domain_phi_mean']['GTNN'],2)}} for communication–language, {{num(G3['domain_phi_mean']['CNXH'],2)}} for personal–social, {{num(G3['domain_phi_mean']['BCHOC'],2)}} for imitation–learning and {{num(G3['domain_phi_mean']['DHBT'],2)}} for other atypical signs. In this cohort, then, monotonicity costs nothing at the domain level and the data favour an essentially additive fusion; at the item level (Section 7.3) the monotone model matched the best comparators while guaranteeing that no item answer can lower the risk.
